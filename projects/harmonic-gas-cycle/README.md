# Harmonic Gas Cycle & Volume-Key Laboratory

## Purpose

This project preserves and develops Kris Watson's proposed gas-cycle model: gases have quiet and electrically excited stages; visible color is treated as a state indicator; static is modeled as stored potential; expansion and compression are mapped as a repeating harmonic cycle; and solar-system-scale gas territories are visualized as nested bubbles.

The project separates:

- established gas-discharge physics;
- thermodynamics and atmospheric science;
- engineering analogy;
- Watson Volume-Key hypothesis;
- symbolic element mapping;
- simulation output.

It does not claim that neon becomes argon, that cooling is only an illusion, or that a static charge is literally nuclear radiation. Those ideas remain project hypotheses and analogies until measured evidence supports them.

## Core two-stage model

### Quiet stage

A gas occupies its ordinary state under the selected pressure, temperature, and composition. It may be invisible even though it remains physically present.

### Electrified stage

Applied electrical energy excites atoms or molecules. When excited states relax, they can emit characteristic wavelengths. Different gases produce different line spectra and therefore different visible colors.

Project interpretation:

```text
quiet volume
→ electrical squeeze
→ visible inner-color stage
→ expansion and relaxation
→ return or next checkpoint
```

## Color ladder

The laboratory compares actual discharge-color references with the project color map.

- Neon: commonly orange-red in discharge tubes.
- Argon: often lavender, violet, or blue-violet depending on pressure, current, and impurities.
- Green: represented as an intermediate project gate, not evidence that neon physically becomes argon.
- Aurora colors: treated separately because they depend largely on excited atmospheric oxygen and nitrogen at different altitudes.

## Volume Key

The Watson Volume Key asks whether an apparent reduction in local temperature can be understood as energy distributed across a larger volume.

The simulator therefore tracks:

```text
energy input
local volume
energy density
pressure proxy
emission intensity
expansion factor
```

A useful simulation relation is:

```text
energy density = stored energy / occupied volume
```

This does not replace thermodynamics. Real systems can transfer energy as heat, radiation, mechanical work, phase change, and escaping particles.

## Static as stored potential

The project uses the phrase “bottled radiation” as an analogy for static charge held by an insulating material. In established physics, static electricity is an imbalance of electric charge and electrical potential. The visualizer shows:

- charge separation;
- dielectric retention;
- leakage rate;
- breakdown threshold;
- spark discharge;
- recovery to equilibrium.

## Neon 10 and Fluorine 9 symbolic loop

The 9/10 sequence is retained as a symbolic containment model:

```text
9 = highly reactive outer drive
10 = inertial grip or gasket
9 ↔ 10 = stored tension loop
```

No physical fluorine handling is proposed. The working visualizer uses safe abstract fields.

## Long-jump and return-flow chart

The site includes a project chart with adjustable values for:

- rise velocity;
- expansion factor;
- local escape tendency;
- return-pressure proxy;
- capture radius;
- restart stage.

These are scenario variables, not replacements for molecular mass, temperature, pressure, gravity, ionization, and solar-wind dynamics.

## Nested solar bubbles

The visualizer maps:

```text
Earth atmosphere
→ magnetosphere
→ solar-wind region
→ heliosphere
→ termination shock
→ heliopause
→ local interstellar medium
```

The project overlays element territories and “rubbing boundaries” to study where pressure, charged particles, and magnetic fields interact.

## Safe energy-harvesting analogies

The laboratory may simulate or measure low-energy effects such as:

- atmospheric electric-field changes;
- small electrostatic generators;
- solar-cell output;
- thermoelectric gradients;
- piezoelectric vibration capture;
- low-voltage inductive coupling;
- environmental RF measurement;
- triboelectric charge.

It must not promise unlimited free energy or bypass conservation of energy.

## Security and safety

- No hazardous gas handling instructions.
- No fluorine, radon, or radioactive-material experiments.
- No high-voltage construction steps.
- No propulsion or weapon implementation.
- No medical claims.
- No secret keys stored in localStorage.
- All saved scenarios must include source, author, timestamp, and claim classification.

## Research questions

- How does discharge color vary with gas identity, pressure, current, and impurities?
- Can the project color ladder help people understand excitation without implying transmutation?
- How closely can charge-retention behavior be represented as a spring or gasket?
- What determines a real atmospheric or heliospheric capture boundary?
- Which parts of the Volume Key correspond to energy density, and which require a new measurable effect?

## Status

This is a speculative scientific visualization and educational project. It is not evidence of gas transmutation, reactionless thrust, or unlimited energy.
